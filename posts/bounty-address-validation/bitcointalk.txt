BITCOINTALK — the announcement thread, as a reply.
==================================================
A forum reader clicked into a technical thread, so the detail is earned here
in a way it is not on a timeline. Three paragraphs longer than the Discord text.

---------------------------------------------------------------

A reader outside this project found the mining pool refusing valid addresses,
and he has been paid for it — the fourth time somebody has done real work for
WAM and taken the coin as the payment for it.

`addressToScript` in `pool/lib/util.js` tested whether an address *looked
like* bech32 before decoding it as base58. That test is a guess: it asks
whether there is a `1` with only bech32 characters after it, and a valid
base58 address answers yes by chance about one time in twenty — 4.99% over
200,000 generated addresses.

This project's own treasury address is one of them. Lower-cased, it has a `1`
followed by 27 characters that all happen to be in the bech32 alphabet, so the
pool called it a segwit address for another chain and refused it — while
base58check decodes it cleanly as this network's P2PKH version.

A miner whose address matched was refused at `mining.authorize` and
disconnected, told his own address belonged to another chain. An operator whose
`poolAddress` matched could not start his pool at all.

He also found what makes this more than one file's mistake: the identical fault
was fixed in the miner on 19 September, in `miner/src/address.h`, and the pool
was never updated with it. It carried its original commit for twenty-six days.

Fixed by decoding first. base58check carries a checksum, so it decides instead
of guessing, and a real segwit address cannot survive it.
`pool/test/address-validation.test.js` holds the order down.

250 WAM, the hardening rung — where he classified it himself. txid
`2ba6139518819ffeee0a15d4075680f04fa00a4730859cbe206ea4b1c57c2d12`, block
21,114 — written out at https://wamcoin.org/security/ and in the treasury
ledger with every other payment.


The measurement, for anyone who wants to repeat it: 200,000 generated
addresses per network and per script type, refused at 4.99% and 5.08% on
mainnet P2PKH and P2SH, and within a tenth of a point of that on testnet and
regtest. The reporter ran 5,000, 1,000 and 200,000 of his own and got 5.04%,
4.8% and 5.12%. It is a property of the alphabets rather than of any one
network: after a `1`, a base58 address has 53 characters of 58 that are also
bech32 characters, case-insensitively.

Why it survived a month: nothing compared the pool's answer to the miner's.
The same decision — which script pays this address — is written in JavaScript
in the pool and in C++ in the miner, and a fix to one had no way of reaching
the other. That is now a check of its own, which reads both and the node, and
fails if any two of them answer differently about any address.

The three before this one are in the same policy, with the same arithmetic
behind each amount: 10,000 for pool accounting in September, 10,000 for share
accounting and 1,000 for the maturation path in October. 21,250 WAM in all.
